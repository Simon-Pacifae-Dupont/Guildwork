Three questions before saying you do not need this

I am not trying to convince you. These three questions are enough, and you already know the answers.

Who, in your company, had an AI do something this week — and where is it written down?

A quote, a customer reply, a meeting summary, a contract clause. If the answer is "in their ChatGPT history", it is written at the vendor's, not at yours.

If that person left tomorrow, what would remain of the way they do it?

The employee who "knows how to handle" the AI has a method. It lives in their head and in their prompts. It leaves with them, like the Excel macros of old.

The last time an AI got something wrong at your company, how did you find out?

From a customer. By chance. Three weeks later. Or — the most frequent answer, and the most honest — you did not.

If the answer to the third is "we did not", you already have the incident. What you are missing is the register — the place where every error becomes a rule instead of happening again.

Six situations you will recognise

None is invented in its mechanism: these are the four failures every team that puts several agents to work produces, carried outside code. Under each, the rule that prevents it. The first is as far from code as one can get.

The restaurant

Tonight's menu was written by an AI. So was the allergen line.

Every week the sous-chef has an assistant write the evening menu from last week's — it is fast, it is clean, and the allergen line comes with it. This week the tiramisu changed: the new recipe has walnuts. The allergen line did not move, because the assistant read the old recipe card. Nobody re-read it; it is "the same menu as usual". The allergic customer is in the dining room on a Tuesday.

This is not an AI error. It is a source that changed without anything saying so, a menu printed without a human who did not write it reading it, and a previous time — there is always one — when the menu was already wrong and nobody wrote it down. The mechanism is exactly the one that breaks a team of developers. It does not need a line of code to exist.

What was missing, in three words

The source, named. Which recipe card was used — this week's or last week's — written on the menu itself, before printing.

The gate. Nothing goes to the dining room until someone who did not write it has read the allergen line, and signed it.

The register. The previous time, written down in one line, would have become the rule that prevents this one.

The rule fits in one sentence, and an owner understands it without being told what an agent is: nothing goes to the dining room unless the card that was used is named, and a human who did not write it has read it.

The firm

Two contradictory answers to the same client

Two associates, two AI assistants, two prompts. The client receives one position on Monday and its opposite on Tuesday, and nobody can say which one was reviewed.

One task, one written contract; nothing leaves without a reviewer who did not write it.

The agency

An invented claim, published under the client's name

A plausible figure, a source that does not exist, a post that has been live for four days. Nobody knows what the agent was asked, or by whom.

A gate before publication; what was used to produce it is named, and kept.

The support desk

A refund promised outside policy

The agent answering tickets promised a goodwill gesture nobody had authorised. Found three weeks later, by accounting.

The agent knows what it may commit to, refuses the rest — and says that it refuses.

The engineering office

A quote computed on last year's price list

The agent read the right template and the wrong price file. The quote went out, signed. The substitution was silent: nothing said which list had been used.

Every output names its sources; a change of source is an event, not a detail.

Management

The method went on leave

The assistant who has the board minutes produced "her way" is away for three weeks. Nobody knows how to reproduce what she got.

Every session leaves a record the next one can read; the method belongs to the company.

You, tonight

The human who carries the messages

You paste the output of one tool into another, all day, so that your agents "work together". You have become the message bus of your own assistants.

Agents hand work over through a board, not through you; you decide, you no longer carry.
